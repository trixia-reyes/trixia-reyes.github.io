Research Interests

My research looks at how Filipino individuals and families cope with caregiving demands, economic stress and work transitions, and at how schools and organizations can support well-being. I work mainly with qualitative, phenomenological designs, complemented by survey-based quantitative and mixed-methods research.

1. Family Well-being and Neurodevelopmental Disorders

The inner, emotional journey of parents raising children with neurodevelopmental disorders, and what it means for family support, psychoeducation and school–home partnerships. Outputs: journal article (2024); oral presentation, 3rd Dasmariñas Research Congress (2026).

2. Work, Job Loss and Economic Stress

The lived experiences of Filipino breadwinners who lost their jobs during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the psychological and family consequences of job loss. Outputs: M.A. thesis (2022); journal article (2024).

3. Leadership, Commitment and Satisfaction in Higher Education

Organizational commitment and satisfaction among student leaders and psychology faculty, used as the basis for a development program. Links to faculty development, student leadership and organizational psychology. Output: journal article (2024).

4. Community and Health Psychology in the Philippine Context

Through thesis supervision: HIV stigma and misconceptions among cisgender women in Quezon City, and the resilience and well-being of farmers after weather-induced agricultural damage in Cagayan Valley. Informed by training in Sikolohiyang Pilipino, culturomics and psychological first aid.

Research methods: phenomenology; interpretative phenomenological analysis (IPA); thematic analysis; in-depth interviews and qualitative coding; survey design; descriptive and inferential statistics; mixed-methods research; psychological testing and assessment; research ethics.

Statistical software: R (RStudio), SPSS, jamovi, JASP.
